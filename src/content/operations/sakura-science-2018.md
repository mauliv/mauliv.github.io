## 1. Bilateral Mandate & Institutional Framework

The **Japan-Asia Youth Exchange Program in Science** (commonly designated the **Sakura Science Exchange Program**, administered specifically for secondary education as the **Sakura Science High School Program / SSHP**) was inaugurated in 2014 by the **Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST)**.

Operated under the strategic sponsorship of:
* **Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science, and Technology (MEXT), Japan**
* **Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA), Japan**
* **Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST)**
* **Kementerian Pendidikan dan Kebudayaan Republik Indonesia (Kemendikbud RI)**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             BILATERAL DIPLOMATIC & SCIENTIFIC MANDATE                   │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Japanese Inter-Ministerial Trust  │ Indonesian Educational Authority   │
│ • Japan Science & Technology (JST)│ • Kemendikbud RI (Dit. Pembinaan   │
│ • MEXT (Education & Science)      │   SMK & Dit. Pembinaan SMA)        │
│ • MOFA (Foreign Affairs)          │ • Embassy of Indonesia, Tokyo      │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
                                    ▼
       ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │   24 National Student Delegates (10 SMK + 14 SMA)       │
       │   8-Day Intensive Frontier Science Immersion in Japan   │
       └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The core objective of the program is to immerse outstanding young minds from Asian nations into Japan's advanced scientific, industrial, and tertiary research environments, promoting bilateral cross-cultural exchange, cultivating early interest in high-technology disciplines, and nurturing lifelong **Young Ambassadors** connected through the worldwide **Sakura Science Club**.

> 🌐 **Foundational Mobility Track:**  
> This Tokyo expedition marked the earliest international milestone of my engineering journey, later culminating in university-level bilateral representations such as the **[Pukyong National University International Summer School (PKNU-ISS)](/international/pknu-iss-2024)** in Busan, South Korea, and five-plus years of institutional operations stewardship at the **[UNY International Office](/international/io-operations)**.

## 2. National Delegation & Selection Rigor

In early 2018, the Ministry of Education and Culture of the Republic of Indonesia (**Kemendikbud RI**), through the Directorate of Vocational High School Development (*Direktorat Pembinaan SMK*) and Directorate of Senior High School Development (*Direktorat Pembinaan SMA*), conducted a competitive nationwide merit selection to nominate Indonesia's official delegates for **SSHP 2018 (Gelombang I / Batch 1)**.

Out of secondary institutions across Indonesia, **24 students** were chosen:
* **10 Vocational High School (SMK) Delegates:** Representing specialized engineering, telecommunications, and industrial design tracks (including SMKN 3 Yogyakarta, SMKN 3 Jombang, SMK Bina Informatika, SMKS Telkom Sandhy Putra, SMKN 55 Jakarta, SMKN 7 Bandung, SMKN 2 Singosari, SMKN 17 Samarinda, SMK Maitreyawira, and SMKN 2 Banjarmasin).
* **14 Academic High School (SMA) Delegates:** Representing natural science and mathematics tracks from leading public and private schools across Java, Sumatra, and Banten.

I was selected representing **SMK Negeri 3 Yogyakarta (Skagata)** from the **Computer and Network Engineering (Teknik Komputer dan Jaringan / TKJ)** department, under official endorsement from the school leadership and faculty advisors.

The delegation was supervised on-site by ministry officials from Kemendikbud RI (Directorate of Vocational High School Development and Directorate of Senior High School Development) in coordination with JST program officers.

## 3. Nobel Laureate Keynote Lecture & High School Exchange

On April 9, 2018, our delegation convened at **Shibaura Institute of Technology Kashiwa Junior & Senior High School** in Chiba Prefecture to attend a keynote lecture delivered by **Prof. Takaaki Kajita**, recipient of the **2015 Nobel Prize in Physics** (jointly awarded for the discovery of neutrino oscillations at the underground Super-Kamiokande detector).

Prof. Kajita walked delegates through the journey of scientific inquiry—from constructing massive sensor arrays 1,000 meters underground in the Kamioka mine to detecting subtle cosmic ray interactions that proved neutrinos have non-zero mass. For a cohort of high school delegates, the lecture provided direct exposure to the patience, experimental rigor, and international collaboration required to drive global breakthrough discoveries.

Following the scientific lecture, the Indonesian delegates participated in a cross-cultural workshop with Shibaura High School students, collaborating on hand-drawn Japanese folding fans (*sensu*) and cultural exchange.

## 4. Deep-Sea Marine & Earth Science: JAMSTEC Yokosuka

On April 10, 2018, the delegation traveled to the headquarters of the **Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology (JAMSTEC)** in Yokosuka, Kanagawa Prefecture. Operating under its governing ethos:
> *"Towards an integrated understanding of the Ocean, the Earth, and Life"*

JAMSTEC conducts world-leading exploration of deep-sea trenches, seismic fault lines, and sub-seafloor biosystems.

Key facilities and scientific initiatives examined:
1. **Manned & Autonomous Submersibles:** Engineering insights into the **Shinkai 6500**, a manned research submersible capable of diving to 6,500 meters beneath the surface to explore extreme abyssal depths, tectonic plate subduction zones, and hydrothermal vent chimneys.
2. **Deep-Sea Drilling Vessel *Chikyu*:** Exploration of scientific ocean drilling technologies designed to reach the Earth's mantle through oceanic crust and retrieve cores directly from seismogenic earthquake generation zones.
3. **Marine Bioscience & Specimen Archives:** Examination of extremophile organisms flourishing in chemoautotrophic deep-sea hydrothermal ecosystems without sunlight, providing crucial data on the origins of life and bio-resource engineering.
4. **Oceanographic Big Data Infrastructure:** Overview of JAMSTEC's open-access data portals that stream real-time sensor networks, bathymetry mappings, and seafloor seismometer telemetry across the Pacific rim.

## 5. Frontier Academic Laboratories & Active Learning: Chiba University

On April 11, 2018, our delegation participated in an immersive full-day scientific program at **Chiba University**, a leading Japanese National University founded in 1949.

The delegates were divided into specialized laboratory visits matching their technical domains. Given my vocational background in **Computer and Network Engineering (TKJ)**, I was placed under the personal mentorship of **Prof. Ken-ya Hashimoto**:

### A. Electro-Communications Laboratory (Prof. Ken-ya Hashimoto)
Located in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Prof. Ken-ya Hashimoto’s laboratory is an internationally renowned center for radio frequency (RF) hardware and acoustic wave device engineering:
* **Surface Acoustic Wave (SAW) & Bulk Acoustic Wave (BAW) Devices:** Investigated how high-frequency acoustic waves propagating on piezoelectric crystals serve as essential bandpass filters and resonators in cellular smartphones, wireless transceivers, and radar telecommunications.
* **Micro-Fabrication & RF Signal Analysis:** Observed cleanroom semiconductor lithography techniques, simulation workflows, and high-frequency vector network analyzers utilized to suppress spurious acoustic resonances in ultra-compact mobile communication front-ends.

The delegation also engaged with other prominent laboratory directors:
* **Prof. Wenwei Yu:** Medical Instrument and Welfare (assistive) instrument systems, focusing on cybernetic prosthetics and rehabilitation robotics.
* **Prof. Hao Liu:** Biomechanical Engineering Laboratory, analyzing flapping-wing aerodynamics and bio-inspired robotic flight.
* **Prof. Josaphat Tetuko Sri Sumantyo:** Center for Environmental Remote Sensing (CEReS), developing synthetic aperture radar (SAR) microwave satellite payloads.

## 6. Metropolitan Disaster Mitigation & Civic Resilience: Sona Area Tokyo

On April 12, 2018, the delegation conducted field training at the **Tokyo Rinkai Disaster Prevention Park ("Sona Area Tokyo")** in Koto City, Tokyo.

This 13.2-hectare facility serves a dual national mandate:
1. **Emergency Command Base:** In the event of a catastrophic Tokyo Inland Earthquake, Sona Area serves as the central command headquarters for the National Government and Tokyo Metropolitan Government, coordinating emergency medical helicopters, military logistics, and regional rescue operations.
2. **Experiential Survival Academy:** During non-disaster periods, the facility educates citizens and emergency personnel on self-help (*swadaya*) and mutual assistance (*gotong royong*).

### The 72-Hour Survival Simulation
Delegates participated in an intensive interactive seismic simulation scenario:
* **The Scenario:** Trapped on the 10th floor of a collapsed cinema complex following a sudden magnitude 7.3 metropolitan earthquake.
* **Navigation Protocol:** Armed with handheld guidance tablets, delegates were required to navigate dark service stairwells, dodge live electrical hazards, assess structural damage, and locate safe perimeter evacuation corridors.
* **Shelter & Resource Management:** The simulation instructed delegates on how to survive the critical first 72 hours before government supply lines reach devastated areas—covering makeshift sanitation systems, water purification, emergency first aid triage, and psychological stability in community evacuation centers.

### A. Miraikan (National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation)
In the afternoon of April 12, 2018, the delegation visited **Miraikan** in Odaiba, Tokyo, operating under the vision:
> *"Open Your Eyes to Science: See a Whole New World"*

The museum presents cutting-edge science as an active civic dialogue between humanity and technology:
* **Honda ASIMO & Humanoid Kinematics:** Watched live demonstrations of Honda’s iconic **ASIMO** robot, analyzing its 34 degrees of freedom, zero-moment point (ZMP) dynamic walking algorithms, balance recovery when navigating uneven surfaces, and voice recognition capabilities.
* **The Geo-Cosmos Display:** Observed the iconic 6-meter digital globe suspended from the museum ceiling, constructed with over 10,000 OLED panels displaying real-time global weather patterns, ocean currents, surface temperatures, and orbital satellite telemetry in 10-million-pixel resolution.

### B. Tokyo Metropolitan University (TMU) & Gold Catalyst Nanotechnology
On April 13, 2018, the delegation visited **Tokyo Metropolitan University (TMU)** in Hachioji. Following an interactive academic seminar on developmental biology and organism metamorphosis, we conducted a specialized tour of the **Gold Catalyst Research Center**:
* **Breakthrough Discovery:** Bulk gold is famously chemically inert and noble. However, pioneering research by TMU’s **Prof. Masatake Haruta** proved that when gold is miniaturized to sub-5 nanometer clusters deposited on metal oxides, it becomes an extraordinarily active heterogeneous catalyst.
* **Low-Temperature Oxidation:** Examined cleanroom synthesis apparatus, electron microscopy suites, and gas reactors demonstrating how gold nanocatalysts oxidize toxic carbon monoxide (CO) at room and sub-zero temperatures, with revolutionary applications in air purification, fuel cells, and environmental safety.

## 10. Reflections & Foundational Engineering Trajectory

For an eleventh-grade vocational student from Yogyakarta who had **never previously boarded an aircraft or traveled outside the island of Java**, the Sakura Science High School Program represented a transformative inflection point in my worldview and technical ambition.

Beyond the sheer technological marvels—such as witnessing humanoid robotics at Miraikan, acoustic wave RF filters at Chiba University, and seismic emergency infrastructure at Sona Area—the deepest lasting impact came from observing **Japan’s institutional discipline, civil order, and educational intentionality**:
* **Active Learning & Systems Rigor:** Observing Chiba University’s *Academic Link* model demonstrated that true intellectual innovation requires seamless infrastructure where physical environments actively encourage curiosity, collaboration, and intense focus.
* **Vocational Engineering Confidence:** Representing Indonesia as a vocational student alongside top academic peers confirmed that hands-on technical skills in networking, hardware, and infrastructure are foundational to global frontier research.
* **Civic Infrastructure Mindset:** Experiencing Japan’s earthquake preparedness and bullet-train logistics instilled an enduring respect for high-reliability systems designed to safeguard human life and function flawlessly under catastrophic stress.

Upon returning to Yogyakarta, this experience immediately catalyzed new engineering initiatives at **SMK Negeri 3 Yogyakarta**, including architecting the school’s first **[Online E-Voting System (OSIS)](/credentials/smk-tkj)** just four months later. In subsequent years, this foundational international perspective directly informed my leadership at the **[UNY International Office](/international/io-operations)** managing bilateral diplomatic delegations and cross-border university partnerships, as well as succeeding in later international academic programs like the **[PKNU International Summer School (Busan, South Korea)](/international/pknu-iss-2024)**.
