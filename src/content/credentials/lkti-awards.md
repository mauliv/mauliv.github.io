## Overview

When Indonesian schools reopened with strict capacity limits following pandemic closures, classes were divided into alternating shifts. Teachers had to repeat the same lessons for morning and afternoon groups, halving live instructional hours for students while doubling preparation workloads.

To solve this operational bottleneck, three of us from Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta formed an interdisciplinary research team to design and evaluate synchronous hybrid learning architectures. Our research placed in two nationwide student scientific writing competitions, supported by official award certificates and national press coverage.

* **Competition:** National Student Scientific Paper Competition 2021
* **Host:** Faculty of Teacher Training and Education, Universitas Lampung
* **Date:** December 4, 2021 (Final defense via Zoom)
* **Theme:** Empowering the Younger Generation for Society 5.0 in the Post-Pandemic Era
* **Result:** 3rd Place National Award

### The Paper and Proposed System
* **Title:** Vircon Classroom: Synchronous Hybrid Learning System
* **Authors:** Co-authored by three of us from Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta across Information Technology, Civic Education, and Automotive Engineering.
* **My Role:** Co-Author & Lead Systems Engineer (designed the synchronous audiovisual workflow and software architecture).

Under the staggered reopening policies, schools ran split shifts or alternating-week rotations. This created uneven learning paces between cohorts and strained school resources.

We designed the Vircon Classroom framework around a synchronous hybrid architecture:
* The teacher conducts class from the physical classroom using standard audio-visual equipment connected to a video conferencing platform such as Zoom or Google Meet.
* In-person students participate on site, while remote students join the live session from home at the same time.
* Cohorts alternate between in-person and virtual attendance on a set schedule, ensuring all students receive equal face-to-face instructional time over the term.
* Teachers evaluate both groups during the same session, avoiding the need to prepare and deliver duplicate lessons across separate shifts.

## 3. Academic Lineage: From Student Papers to Working EdTech

These early writing competitions helped establish my ongoing focus on educational tools and classroom validation:
* **From conceptual papers to classroom prototypes:** The experience investigating learning barriers carried directly into [Plant Escape AR](/it/plant-escape-ar), a mobile augmented reality game I engineered to help junior high school students study plant biology without dedicated laboratory microscopes.
* **National grants and recognition:** Plant Escape AR earned funding through the Student Creativity Program (PKM-RSH), advanced as a national finalist at PIMNAS 35 in 2022, and received two official copyright registrations from the Indonesian Ministry of Law and Human Rights.
* **Peer-reviewed publication:** Classroom evaluation data we gathered from school trials was later published in Jurnal Informatika dan Rekayasa Perangkat Lunak (JATIKA, Vol. 3 No. 4, December 2022).
* **Undergraduate research:** The empirical evaluation methods I learned while testing these educational projects culminated in my Bachelor of Engineering thesis using WebQual 4.0 and Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA) on UNY's international student mobility platform ([SIMOBY UNY](/it/simoby)).
